# Client passivity — Final Amended Plan (panel synthesis, 2026-07-26)

## Verdict on the owner theory

**Sound with amendments.** The theory's substrate is real and confirmed: the §3 liveness diagnosis is exactly right in code (CP26), the settlement/overlay convergence machine, servability classifier, doc-set delivery path, per-session negotiation seam, scoped-lane egress gates, and push-side isolation are all sound for what P0–P5 ask of them (CP24, CP25, CP27–CP31), and the two structural attacks — mixed-fleet double execution (CP5) and foreign-scoped-instance leakage into speculation closures (CP16) — are refuted by existing mechanism. But three of the theory's load-bearing sentences are false as written: "the canonical value always arrives from the server" fails for the entire dynamic fail-open class, whose client rerun is canonical *by design* (CP1); "served canonical values ride the same accepted-commit push" is mechanically false — they arrive on a second, later refresh wave behind settle and a load-inflating drain-wait (CP3); and "at most one extra round" holds only for non-conflicting interleaves (CP7). P5 as skeleton-written ("retire the closure pull path") contradicts the plan's own fallback, offline, and settlement-gate requirements (CP1, CP2, CP9), boot parity is unproven for session-rank state and structurally at risk for k-concurrent boots (CP4), and the effect registry feeding the never-speculate-effects line has two builtins on the wrong side (CP6). None of this overturns the end state. It converts parity from an unconditional claim into a **conditional one — warm-path/speculation-hit parity plus measured, owner-ratified budgets for the miss, cold, conflict, crossing, and fail-open tails** — and it restructures P3/P4/P5 accordingly. The end acceptance bar is retained verbatim.
